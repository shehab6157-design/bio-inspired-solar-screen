Chameleon mode

A chameleon changes its skin to its surroundings. This screen reflects room light like paper when it's bright, and glows like a normal phone screen when it's dim. Compare it with a phone that only glows and a screen that only reflects.

Phones and TVs: taking off the sunglasses

Every OLED screen wears a polarizer, like sunglasses: it stops glare but throws away more than half the screen's own light. Take it off and glare comes back. A moth-eye surface removes that glare. Choose the layers and the room light, and see whether the screen still beats the glare and what it saves.

Two Seas: an hour in the summer sun

Sunlight puts several watts of heat into a phone's face, more than its own chip. Phones answer by dimming or shutting down. A heat mirror sends the invisible heat back while visible light passes, like two seas that meet but do not mix. Moon mode goes further: like the moon, the screen shows the picture with the sun's own light.

The hive: a home full of screens

In a bee colony there is no boss: each bee takes work when it is well fed and declines it when it is tired. Here the phone, watch, TV and five small light-powered screens around a home share the messages the same way. These are real results: 30 simulated homes, January 2020 sunlight, no evening lamp.
